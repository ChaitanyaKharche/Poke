Do Pokémon singles fall in Sept to Dec and recover in Jan to Apr?

498 cards (top 15 by price from 37 English sets, 1999 to 2025), monthly ungraded prices from PriceCharting, Jan 2021 to Sep 2026. Each card's first 12 months after release are excluded so new-set drops don't count as seasonal.

1. The two seasons, year by year

Average price change across the basket. Left: what prices actually did. Right: the same windows after removing each year's overall trend.

Jan to AprSep to Dec

Mostly no. Sep to Dec fell in only 2 of 5 years (2021, 2023). After removing the trend, Jan to Apr beat Sep to Dec in 3 of 5 years, which is close to a coin flip.

2. Which months run weak

Each month's move versus that year's average month. Bar = average of all years, dots = individual years.

AverageOne year

The soft stretch is Jun to Nov, not Sep to Dec. July and September were below trend in 5 of 6 years. January was above trend in 4 of 5 years, but its +2.4% average leans on Jan 2025 (+8.4%). With 12 months tested over only 5 to 6 years, a couple will look consistent by chance, so read this as a lean, not a rule.

3. What actually hits new cards: release decay

Every card worth $5+ in three 2026 sets (TCGplayer data from your API). Median change from the price ~2 to 3 weeks after release.

Perfect Order (Mar 27)Chaos Rising (May 22)Pitch Black (Jul 17)

This pattern is real and strong. Pitch Black chase cards were down 34% by day 60, Chaos Rising 60% by day 120, Perfect Order 51% by day 180. By day 120, every chase card in Chaos Rising and Perfect Order was below its starting price. It happened in spring and summer alike, so it is not a season.

Caveats

  • Only about 5.5 years of history exist on PriceCharting, so there are 5 Sep to Dec windows. That is too few to prove or rule out a small seasonal effect.
  • The basket is today's top 15 cards per set, which favours cards that went up. The trend removal limits how much that matters for seasonality.
  • PriceCharting points behave like monthly average prices (checked against TCGplayer daily history: about 7% error vs 11% for the price on the 1st), so a "month" here is average to average.
  • 2021 includes the end of the COVID boom, and 2024 to 2026 a strong bull run. Those swings are far bigger than any calendar effect.
